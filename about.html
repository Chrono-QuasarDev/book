Our Story

Four decades on
one stretch of coast.

Three generations, one family, and a building that was never supposed to be a hotel.

1983 — Today

How it happened.

1983

A cannery, and a bad idea

Teresa Almeida bought the ruined sardine cannery on the headland for less than the cost of the roof it did not have. Everyone told her the road was too poor and the cliff too steep. She kept the walls and started again.

1991

Six rooms and a kitchen table

The first guests were friends of friends who paid what they could. Breakfast was served at one long table, which is a habit the resort has never entirely broken — Solane still keeps a chef's table in the same spot.

2004

The pool that took three years

Cutting an infinity pool into living rock is slower than anyone budgets for. It opened two summers late and immediately became the photograph that sells the place — which nobody had planned for either.

2019

Solane earns its star

Teresa's granddaughter Inês came home from Copenhagen, threw out the menu, and rebuilt it entirely from what the bay produces. The star arrived fourteen months later. The sardines, fittingly, are still on the menu.

Today

Forty-two suites, still family-run

We have added a spa, a beach club and a great deal of glass, and removed nothing that mattered. Sixty-one per cent of guests come back — which is the only review we have ever really cared about.
